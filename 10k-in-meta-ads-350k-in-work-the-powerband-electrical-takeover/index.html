$10K in Meta Ads. $350K in work. The Powerband Electrical takeover.

Nexevo Digital TeamMarch 19, 20263 min readCase Study

Powerband Electrical came to us the way a lot of good businesses do: plenty of skill on the tools, a reputation that earned repeat work, and a marketing setup that was leaving most of the opportunity on the table. The website was getting around 500 visits a month. Paid advertising had never really been given a proper go. Growth was happening, but it was happening by word of mouth, at word-of-mouth speed.

The brief was a full takeover. Not one channel, not a quick campaign, the whole engine: Meta Ads, SEO, and the website itself, all built and run by one team with one scoreboard.

The headline number

Over the campaign period, roughly $10,000 of Meta Ads spend produced $350,000 in tracked work for Powerband. That is not a projection or a feel-good estimate. Every inquiry was tracked from the ad it came from through to the job it became, which is the only honest way to measure advertising.

$10,000 in ad spend. $350,000 in tracked revenue. Every dollar accounted for from click to invoice.

Numbers like that do not come from boosting posts and hoping. They come from treating Meta as a system: audiences built on Powerband’s own data first, creative made from real jobs rather than stock polish, retargeting sequences that answered the hesitations people actually had, and relentless weekly testing on one metric, cost per qualified lead. When a winning angle showed itself, it got the budget. When creative started to fatigue, it was replaced before performance sagged.

Meanwhile, the traffic engine

Paid ads win the week. Search wins the years. While the Meta campaigns were filling the calendar, the SEO and website work was compounding underneath: monthly traffic went from 500 visitors to 5,000 in six months. A tenfold lift, and the kind that keeps paying after the work is done, because rankings do not switch off when a campaign ends.

That growth came from the unglamorous fundamentals done properly. The site was rebuilt to be fast, clear and structured the way search engines reward. Service pages were written for the searches customers actually type, not the jargon of the trade. Technical health, internal linking and genuine authority building did the rest, month after month.

The part most people miss

The website rebuild was not just an SEO play. It was the conversion layer that made every other channel cheaper. Before the rebuild, visitors arrived and too many of them left without inquiring. After it, the same traffic produced far more inquiries, which means the Meta budget worked harder, the rankings paid better, and every future marketing dollar got a head start.

This is the whole argument for a full takeover instead of a channel here and a freelancer there. Ads, search and the website are not three separate projects. They are one system, and when the same team runs all of it, every improvement feeds the others. The ad data tells the SEO team what customers want. The SEO content gives the ads better pages to land on. The website turns both streams of traffic into booked work.

The results, in one place

  • $10,000 in Meta Ads spend turned into $350,000 of tracked work
  • Monthly website traffic up from 500 visitors to 5,000 in six months
  • A rebuilt website converting visitors into inquiries at multiples of the old rate
  • Full tracking from first click to booked job, so every number above is provable
  • No lock-in contract at any point. The results are why Powerband stays.
